01

Why resolution mattered

The obvious imagery source — Sentinel-2, freely available at 10m/pixel — turned out to be unusable for the actual goal. A small azud is one or two pixels wide at that scale. Switching to IGN's real PNOA aerial imagery (25–50cm/pixel) made the difference between a pipeline that can only re-confirm known large dams, and one that can find the small, undocumented ones this project exists to find.

Same real reservoir, same real coordinates — the only difference is the imagery source.

02

Finding dams it had never seen

A detector was fine-tuned on just 26 hand-verified examples from the proof-of-concept area, then tested against 12 held-out dams excluded entirely from training. It correctly found 11 of 12 — including cases like these, where the model draws a tight box around the real structure with no prior exposure to it.

03

What this proves — and what it doesn't

Proves: the core method works end-to-end on real data — real ground truth, real imagery, real held-out validation, not a simulation. The free-tier, small-team approach the brief was betting on is technically viable.

Doesn't yet prove: this is one detection layer (of four planned), on one 15×15km area (of a 12,000km² basin), trained on 26 examples with a fast heuristic labeling shortcut rather than the full manual annotation process. No precision/recall figure at basin scale exists yet, and the DEM-based layer — the plan's highest-risk piece — is still waiting on an external researcher's reply.

This was scoped deliberately small on short notice, specifically to get an honest answer to "does this approach work at all" before investing days of build time basin-wide. It does.
